The first three seconds decide if a reel gets watched.

A reel doesn't get judged on its ending — it gets judged in the first three seconds, before most viewers have consciously decided to keep watching. That's the window a hook has to work in.

The strongest hooks do one of three things immediately: promise a specific outcome, interrupt an expectation, or open a visible gap the viewer wants closed. A vague opening line, even a well-shot one, loses to a blunt one that gets straight to the point.

A simple hook framework

Start with the payoff, not the setup. Show the result first, then explain how you got there. It sounds backwards, but it's why "day 1 vs day 30" style reels consistently outperform a slow build-up.

Editing can only do so much — the hook has to be right in the script before a single clip gets cut.
